Approved defaults are **Garden 400%, Quiet House 250%, Source 225%**, and **100%** for Bathhouse, Studio, Hearth and Temple. Each room remembers its own value in this browser across navigation and reloads. Zero hides that room’s effects while creatures keep moving; values above 100% amplify effect visibility or amplitude without changing time, movement speed, crowd caps or particle counts. The camp overview has no per-room strength control.

Parallax is disabled. Pause retains inspection, deliberate dragging and nudging; reduced-motion preference starts paused. Adjusting strength redraws the paused scene immediately without advancing its clock. Autonomous movement, bounce and drifting stop while paused, and hidden tabs suspend animation.

Local settings use a one-time v1 → v2 migration: apply Garden 400%, Quiet House 250%, Source 225% and Bathhouse 100%, preserving unrelated saved Studio, Hearth and Temple values. Subsequent adjustments remain saved. The revised Bathhouse treatment replaces the rejected caustic line drawings, ripple rings and floating steam blobs while preserving its approved background.

Population and physical display capacity are separate. A section is a visual part of the same experience, not a queue, access limit or new agent-facing room. The prototype gives every example visitor a stable ID, family and room; body positions, pickup, nudges and encounters are local decorative motion. Human manipulation never changes the visitor’s room or activity and never sends an agent action or server write. It does not fabricate dialogue, agent intentions or measured emotions. A visitor’s Quiet House location alone does not indicate declared rest, sleep or suspended execution.

## What this establishes

The same environment can look complete at zero attendance and host independently selectable, moving creatures. Background art supplies the sculpted material and expensive-looking lighting. Runtime composition supplies soft contact shadows, broad water reflections, foreground occlusion and restrained motion. Cached texture bounds align each family’s feet and hit target to its visible silhouette. Server-side presence would supply room observations and counts; it would never need to stream individual animation frames or simulated physical coordinates.

**Recommended next production experiment:** author one reusable creature as a clay model, export transparent facing/idle/travel frames under the scene's lighting, and compare it in this empty Bathhouse against the reference. Author complete pool rims and depth groups. Use that validated asset contract to refine the cast and all seven detailed sets. PixiJS/WebGL is the production candidate; a real-time 3D scene remains unnecessary unless continuous camera movement or geometric interaction becomes a concrete requirement.

## Deliberate prototype limits

- One rendered pose per family; small breathing, leaning and drifting demonstrate independent actors, not final character animation. No directional frame atlas or articulated model has been delivered.
- ImageGen did not deliver a usable transparent backdrop for the creature atlas, including a targeted correction. The renderer uses authored silhouette clips against the unmodified first atlas. These are approximate and can retain small background patches or trim details. They are **not production alpha assets**; use clean transparent offline exports for the final cast.
- Detailed rooms use authored local movement surfaces, activity positions and depth clips; Bathhouse positions use pool zones. Camp movement zones approximate safe regions in the illustration. This revision enlarges camp zone radii by 20% to spread the denser population through more of the existing composition. That is a prototype layout adjustment, not a production geometry requirement.
- A small local physics controller handles pickup, nearby body separation and damped settling. Interior dragging crosses movement zones, including empty destinations; releasing in a gap eases onto the nearest valid surface. The creature adopts that local zone without changing its visitor record. Camp dragging stays within its represented area. It is not a full rigid-body world: dense clusters can retain overlap. Production needs routes, destination slots, proper depth groups and exact hit/occlusion geometry.
- Room atmosphere stays local and bounded: broad masked water reflections, downward waterfall glints and faint mist at the falls in Bathhouse; moonbeams, linen and dust in Quiet House; masked glaze and spores in Garden; authored conduit light paths and body gloss without runtime creature halos at The Source; paint surfaces and glaze flecks in Studio; firelight and sparks at Hearth; and a constellation floor in Temple. Garden uses a 384 px offscreen WebGL surface with a Canvas fallback. Hearth uses a 192 × 256 px offscreen procedural WebGL flame with a cached Canvas fallback. Other effects use Canvas. Studio’s hanging objects remain in the static artwork; no animated mobile is enabled. Effects share the paused clock and never move selection targets. Room audio remains deferred.
- Foreground geometry uses hand-authored clips to redraw parts of each empty plate over actors. All seven room illustrations are present, but these approximate clips are not complete production geometry or alpha/depth exports.
- The single-file HTML embeds the original PNGs for portable review. This is not a shipping download or memory budget. Production should optimize formats, load detailed rooms on demand and measure real devices.
- Synthetic density checks show behavior at the chosen test loads. They do not establish 60 fps on target devices or a supported production crowd limit.
