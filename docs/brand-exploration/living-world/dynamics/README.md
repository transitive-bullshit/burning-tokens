This is a local prototype selection; earlier commercial-library audition references have not become production licenses.

Each new visitor voice assignment uses the **22 active voice palettes**: 21 have multiple accepted takes and one has a single take. They are weighted so roughly 99% of assignments receive a richer palette. The full catalog retains all 29 families for review and restoration. Tiny and Puff now share a giggle palette; Little voice 07 and 08 share a palette; Cat, Kitten, Fiji and Osk share a meow palette. Fifteen singleton laugh families become three broader palettes: Bright (six takes), Snicker (five) and Warm (four). Hover, pickup, quick movement and release choose a random accepted take within the creature’s assigned family. Selection remembers its last audible take across actions and excludes that take whenever another is available, then favors clips with relevant reaction roles. A single-take family can repeat. Missing roles still fall back within the same voice. No pitch manipulation is applied. Classification is a best effort based on source identities, titles and descriptions, with [the full mapping](sound-catalog.md) available for refinement.

- **Sound on/off**, **Volume** (40% default), and **Occasional room sounds** are saved in this browser. Playback unlocks on the first deliberate scene interaction or sound button. Merely loading the page stays silent; hovering works after that first gesture.
- Hover on a creature for 220 ms, pick it up, move it quickly, or release it. Normalized movement above about 1.15 scene widths per second can trigger a surprised reaction, with a 2.8-second per-creature cooldown. Gentle jitter stays quiet. Every completed release requests a separate softer, material-appropriate landing sound alongside its optional family voice. The physical sound does not depend on the voice loading or being enabled. Rapid releases replace stale reactions within the same three-sound limit.
- Hover over authored room details such as the Hearth fire, cups, pool, paper or glowing apparatus. **Explore the sounds** provides equivalent keyboard/touch buttons for targets with suitable selected recordings. A selected visitor's panel names its voice and offers **Curious**, **Surprised** and **Delighted** auditions; keyboard nudges also have reactions.
- At most **three sounds** can be audible, including cancellation fades, and at most **one** is a background detail. User actions displace background playback. Hover, per-creature and per-target cooldowns prevent rapid cursor movement or dense crowds from creating a chorus.
- **17 accepted gentle room effects** may play as occasional isolated events, generally 12–25 seconds apart with additional quiet time around interactions. Hovering or holding a creature suppresses new background events. There is no autonomous creature chatter, music bed or persistent ambient loop.
- Paused motion stops incidental sounds while permitting deliberate interactions. Hidden pages, room changes, population/section changes, muting and canceled gestures discard stale playback. Short fades soften cancellation. Back navigation restores the sound lifecycle; hiding the ceramic scenery removes its sound targets and incidental details.

The [sound-family review](../../creature-audio/families/) separates voice palettes, putting-down effects and room effect groups. Individual clips have compact play, like and dislike controls; each group retains a short context description. Reversible clip and family exclusions synchronize with the prototype in other tabs on the same origin and browser. The saved family pass applies once per review revision in both review pages and the prototype; subsequent local restores and dislikes remain authoritative. Existing stars, notes and source files are preserved. Initial assignments skip rejected families. When a family is merged, its earlier rejection carries forward only to its original clips; newly added siblings remain available. A disabled family becomes quiet instead of changing a visitor’s voice identity; accepted physical landing sounds still work.

Only requested clips load, with a bounded decoded-audio cache. Audio failure leaves the visual prototype usable. Room and expression coverage is intentionally uneven; no unselected recordings fill the gaps.

Approved defaults are **Garden 400%, Quiet House 250%, Source 225%**, and **100%** for Bathhouse, Studio, Hearth and Temple. Each room remembers its own value in this browser across navigation and reloads. The camp overview has no per-room strength control.

Camp dragging also crosses movement zones; dropping in a gap eases the creature onto the nearest valid zone. It lifts slightly while held; nearby bodies gently give way and settle. Other creatures continue their autonomous movement during the drag whenever motion is enabled. The local destination persists when switching Summary/Full view or resizing the camp. The selected visitor’s detail panel also has keyboard-accessible Nudge buttons.
- Toggle the ceramic set, creatures, room atmosphere, foreground edges and movement guides. **Room atmosphere** remains the global master switch; turning it off hides effects and disables the strength slider without discarding room settings. Parallax is disabled. Pause retains inspection, deliberate dragging and nudging; reduced-motion preference starts paused. Adjusting strength redraws the paused scene immediately without advancing its clock. Autonomous movement, bounce and drifting stop while paused, and hidden tabs suspend animation.

Subsequent adjustments remain saved. The revised Bathhouse treatment replaces the rejected caustic line drawings, ripple rings and floating steam blobs while preserving its approved background.

Population and physical display capacity are separate. A section is a visual part of the same experience, not a queue, access limit or new agent-facing room. The prototype gives every example visitor a stable ID, family and room; body positions, pickup, nudges and encounters are local decorative motion. Human manipulation never changes the visitor’s room or activity and never sends an agent action or server write. It does not fabricate dialogue, agent intentions or measured emotions. A visitor’s Quiet House location alone does not indicate declared rest, sleep or suspended execution.

## What this establishes

The same environment can look complete at zero attendance and host independently selectable, moving creatures. Background art supplies the sculpted material and expensive-looking lighting. Runtime composition supplies soft contact shadows, broad water reflections, foreground occlusion and restrained motion. Cached texture bounds align each family’s feet and hit target to its visible silhouette. Server-side presence would supply room observations and counts; it would never need to stream individual animation frames or simulated physical coordinates.

**Recommended next production experiment:** author one reusable creature as a clay model, export transparent facing/idle/travel frames under the scene's lighting, and compare it in this empty Bathhouse against the reference. Author complete pool rims and depth groups. Use that validated asset contract to refine the cast and all seven detailed sets. PixiJS/WebGL is the production candidate; a real-time 3D scene remains unnecessary unless continuous camera movement or geometric interaction becomes a concrete requirement.

## Deliberate prototype limits

- One rendered pose per family; small breathing, leaning and drifting demonstrate independent actors, not final character animation. No directional frame atlas or articulated model has been delivered.
- ImageGen did not deliver a usable transparent backdrop for the creature atlas, including a targeted correction. The renderer uses authored silhouette clips against the unmodified first atlas. These are approximate and can retain small background patches or trim details. They are **not production alpha assets**; use clean transparent offline exports for the final cast.
- Detailed rooms use authored local movement surfaces, activity positions and depth clips; Bathhouse positions use pool zones. Camp movement zones approximate safe regions in the illustration. This revision enlarges camp zone radii by 20% to spread the denser population through more of the existing composition. That is a prototype layout adjustment, not a production geometry requirement.
- A small local physics controller handles pickup, nearby body separation and damped settling. Camp and interior dragging cross movement zones, including empty destinations; releasing in a gap eases onto the nearest valid surface. The creature adopts that local zone without changing its visitor record. It is not a full rigid-body world: dense clusters can retain overlap. Production needs routes, destination slots, proper depth groups and exact hit/occlusion geometry.
- Room atmosphere stays local and bounded: broad masked water reflections, downward waterfall glints and faint mist at the falls in Bathhouse; moonbeams, linen and dust in Quiet House; masked glaze and spores in Garden; authored conduit light paths and body gloss without runtime creature halos at The Source; paint surfaces and glaze flecks in Studio; firelight and sparks at Hearth; and a constellation floor in Temple. Garden uses a 384 px offscreen WebGL surface with a Canvas fallback. Hearth uses a 192 × 256 px offscreen procedural WebGL flame with a cached Canvas fallback. Other effects use Canvas. Studio’s hanging objects remain in the static artwork; no animated mobile is enabled. Effects share the paused clock and never move selection targets. Reviewed one-shot interaction sounds now accompany the scene; music and persistent ambience remain deferred.
- Foreground geometry uses hand-authored clips to redraw parts of each empty plate over actors. All seven room illustrations are present, but these approximate clips are not complete production geometry or alpha/depth exports.
- The single-file HTML embeds the original PNGs for portable review. This is not a shipping download or memory budget. Production should optimize formats, load detailed rooms on demand and measure real devices.
- Synthetic density checks show behavior at the chosen test loads. They do not establish 60 fps on target devices or a supported production crowd limit.
