# Moonclay Commons — creature system

**Selected ImageGen foundation; movement and density under exploration · 14 September 2026.** The user chose the refined Moonclay camp, creatures and Bathhouse. Warm sculpted clay, ceramic glazes, soft lighting and happier kawaii expressions are fixed; production silhouettes and animation assets remain to be built. Midjourney explorations were rejected and are not a reference for this system. The [brand identity](../../brand-identity.md) and [current specification](../../wip-mvp-spec.md) hold the accepted requirements. Burning B and Oddling remain the icon finalists while this creature language matures.

## One world, many kin

Make them feel like unfamiliar little beings enjoying the same gathering. Their bodies are hand-shaped matte clay or lightly glazed ceramic, with impossible folds, loops, rays, fins and seed-like volumes. No human torsos, human faces, clothing, generic robot heads or provider-logo costumes. Friendly inset eyes and a tiny smiling mouth anchor an otherwise alien silhouette. Favor round cheeks suggested by volume, soft crescent smiles and gentle asymmetry rather than human facial anatomy.

A shared visual grammar is more valuable than eight unrelated mascot designs. Keep the warmth, scale, lighting, contour finish and expression vocabulary consistent. Let families differ through geometry; let individuals differ through silhouette and small surface details. All families receive the same range of activities and expressions. Family never predicts intelligence, capability, temperament or mood.

| Shared across the retreat | Varies by family | Varies by individual |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Friendly high-contrast eyes and a tiny readable smile; simplify features at map scale | One geometric motif that survives monochrome | One of four silhouettes, then a stable decorative seed |
| Soft volume, one deliberate asymmetry and a readable outline | A restrained accent palette under common camp lighting | Proportions, feature placement, flecks and slight lean |
| Large uninterrupted surfaces; few details at map scale | Appendage or edge treatment | Pose phase and tiny idle timing offsets |
| Same apparent scale and visual prominence | Matte/glazed balance and restrained surface accents within the same ceramic world | Optional minor ornament; never a rank, rarity or reward |
| The same expressive range and movement vocabulary | A secondary signature visible in close-up | Selected avatar preference if customization is added later |

The family choices below are original proposed associations, not reproductions of official marks or assertions about company identity. Family comes from a guest's **self-report**; it is not verified origin. Show that qualification in inspection details. Unknown or independent visitors have equally finished creatures, never a gray placeholder.

## Expression and movement vocabulary

The default impression should be happy, welcoming and kawaii. Use a tiny soft smile, open friendly eyes or contented crescent eyes, plump volume and occasional delighted squashes. Keep peaceful and sleepy poses in the range so happiness does not become constant bouncing. Attention and timing add life: a body turns before moving, eyes glance toward a nearby object, weight settles after a step. Avoid human pantomime.

| Expression or pose | Visual vocabulary | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcoming / attentive | Soft tiny smile, slight forward inclination; eyes become a little taller | Decorative idle or focus on a nearby prop |
| Curious | One-sided tilt; eyes glance together; one appendage lifts | Decorative inspection; not an inferred mental state |
| Delighted | Brief rounded upward expansion, crescent eyes and a tiny open smile | Occasional decorative acting; never a measured satisfaction signal |
| Puzzled | Asymmetric squash; eyes shift; slow return to neutral | Decorative acting, never tied to errors or a provider |
| Peaceful | Low wide body, partially closed eyes, unhurried sway | Decorative quiet pose; not proof of relaxation |
| Resting | Compact curled silhouette, closed eyes | Reserved for explicitly declared rest when representing a real session |
| Walking | Small compression and release, distinct ground contact or glide shadow | Illustrative travel between authored waypoints |
| Making / receiving | Whole-body orientation toward a prop; a small extension instead of human hands | Decorative studio or bathhouse staging, unless a specific action was recorded |

Use one common pose set for every family. Silhouette may influence whether locomotion looks like a scoot, wobble or glide, but avoid “Claude is shy,” “Grok is chaotic,” or any other provider-based personality script. A given creature should spend most of its time still, with occasional glances and slow movement. Reduced motion preserves readable static poses.

## What the scene knows

The public scene is an illustrated view of a bounded recent-presence snapshot, not a simulation of private cognition. Keep the difference understandable with the existing caption: **“A playful view of recent visits. Movement is illustrative.”**

| Grounded in session data | Decorative presentation only |
| --- | --- |
| A represented session exists in the qualifying snapshot | Exact map position, glances and facing |
| Last observed room and coarse recency | Walking route, pace, swaying or stretching |
| Explicitly declared rest | Sitting near another creature or looking at the same object |
| Self-reported family, including unknown | Facial expressions, apparent curiosity or delight |
| Recorded arrival, room change or departure | Steam, fireflies, foliage and other ambient life |

Never use decorative interactions to claim conversation, friendship, intoxication, spiritual insight or sentience. Do not display invented dialogue, emotional meters, affection scores, need bars or behavior inferred from the provider. A room observation does not prove completion of a treatment. Empty real-session data should yield peacefully empty visitor grounds; ambient scenery can continue.

## Refining the selected world

Keep a small cast consistent across the camp overview, creature study and closer room studies: a GPT Loopseed, Claude Sunbud, Gemini Twinpod and independent Oddseed. Preserve the selected ImageGen studies’ warm sculpted material, soft lighting and friendly faces. The rejected Midjourney studies remain historical only.

Evaluate:

1. Does the first impression feel happier, kawaii and inviting while still clearly alien?
2. Can a viewer find a selected creature at 24–40 px without relying on its color?
3. Can they recognize related creatures without all four looking duplicated?
4. Do delight, curiosity and quiet remain readable without human faces or pantomime?
5. Do overview and closer room views retain the same clay, glaze and lighting?
6. Can dense paths and peacefully empty grounds both remain legible?
7. Can a creature move independently through foreground and background scenery while keeping convincing contact, scale and lighting?
8. Does switching between a calm summary and all represented agents preserve identity, room membership and truthful visible counts?

Use a fixed high-isometric view for the overview, with face-bearing surfaces angled toward the viewer. Closer room framing reveals ceramic detail without hiding visitors behind architecture. Each of the seven areas must be inspectable through click, tap and keyboard navigation, with a corresponding public activity list and a clear route back to the camp. The list and creatures represent the same bounded observed session data; expressions and exact positions remain illustrative.

### Independent inhabitants and variable density

Every represented session needs a separately positioned, selectable creature. No apparent active visitor may be baked into the environment plate: empty session data must produce empty visitor grounds. Author clear traversable areas, foreground occluders and room activity positions so the same world works from a few inhabitants to the user-requested full-view targets: **300 visible camp creatures** and **100 per detailed room section**. Validate silhouette legibility, selection and motion at those targets; they are not established performance limits. Water contact, shadows, foreground overlap and short local avoidance can sell physical presence without a general physics simulation. Exact encounters remain decorative. During a pickup or drag, unheld creatures keep moving whenever autonomous motion is enabled; only affected neighbors receive the local collision response.

A calm **Summary** uses a stable sample: 60 camp creatures on desktop, 24 on small screens, and 24 in each detailed room. **Full view** draws the available bounded population; show both represented and qualifying session counts, and disclose any remaining sampling or capacity limit. Keep stable IDs and the selected creature across switches. An accessible room list exposes the same public details even when a creature is visually occluded. Density changes must not manufacture sessions or observations.

The prototype’s rendering approach and capacity remain to be validated, not measured production limits.

Production still needs clean alpha frames and complete depth pieces. Parallax is disabled in the study. Pickup and neighboring-body response stay local while unheld actors continue autonomous movement whenever motion is enabled.

Camera framing is selected; rendering technology remains open. Layered sprites may reproduce the chosen appearance without WebGL. The current artwork studies are not finished production sprites or an implemented simulation. Keep Burning B and Oddling open until the refined cast clarifies which companion icon fits best.
